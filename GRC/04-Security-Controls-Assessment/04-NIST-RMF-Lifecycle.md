NIST Risk Management Framework (RMF) Lifecycle

1. Project Overview

This document describes how the NIST Risk Management Framework (RMF) could be applied to a fictional UAE-based IT services company, GulfTech Solutions.

The objective is to document a structured approach to identifying, assessing, managing, and monitoring information-system security risks.

Important: This is an educational exercise using a fictional organisation. No real system was assessed, and no control effectiveness or authorisation decision is claimed.

2. System Description

* Organisation: GulfTech Solutions (fictional)
* System: GulfTech Client Services Environment
* Purpose: Supports employee collaboration and access to client documents.
* Environment: Cloud productivity services and employee endpoints.
* Information: Client documents, employee account information, and internal business records.

The actual architecture, information sensitivity, regulatory obligations, and business impact would need to be confirmed in a real assessment.

3. The Seven RMF Steps

Step 1: Prepare

Purpose: Establish the context and responsibilities for managing security and privacy risks.

Proposed activities:

* Identify system stakeholders and responsible owners.
* Define the system boundary and dependencies.
* Establish risk assessment assumptions.
* Identify applicable business, legal, and security requirements.

Expected output: Scope statement, stakeholder list, and assumptions register.

Step 2: Categorize

Purpose: Categorize the system and its information according to potential confidentiality, integrity, and availability impacts.

Proposed activities:

* Identify information processed, stored, or transmitted.
* Consider potential impact if information is disclosed, modified, or unavailable.
* Document the rationale for the proposed categorization.
* Obtain confirmation from the appropriate business and system owners.

Expected output: System categorization worksheet.

No final impact classification is assigned in this educational exercise because the necessary business context has not been validated.

Step 3: Select

Purpose: Select appropriate security and privacy controls based on system categorization, risk, and applicable requirements.

Proposed activities:

* Identify the applicable control baseline or organisational control set.
* Select and tailor controls to the system.
* Assign responsibility for each control.
* Document selection decisions and planned evidence.

Expected output: Draft security control selection and responsibility matrix.

Step 4: Implement

Purpose: Implement selected controls and document how they operate.

Proposed activities:

* Document policies, procedures, and technical configurations.
* Assign control responsibilities.
* Record implementation status and supporting evidence.
* Track exceptions and corrective actions.

Expected output: Control implementation plan.

This project does not claim that the proposed controls have actually been implemented.

Step 5: Assess

Purpose: Determine whether controls are implemented correctly and operate as intended.

Proposed activities:

* Define assessment procedures.
* Review policies, configurations, records, and test results.
* Document findings and limitations.
* Track deficiencies and remediation actions.

Expected output: Assessment plan and findings register.

Actual control effectiveness cannot be established without appropriate evidence.

Step 6: Authorize

Purpose: Enable the designated authorising official to make a risk-based decision about whether a system may operate.

Proposed activities:

* Review assessment results and remaining risks.
* Document proposed risk acceptance decisions.
* Present findings to the authorised decision-maker.
* Record the decision and any required conditions.

Expected output: Authorisation decision template.

No real authorisation decision has been made as part of this project.

Step 7: Monitor

Purpose: Maintain ongoing awareness of security and privacy risks.

Proposed activities:

* Establish control monitoring and review schedules.
* Track incidents, vulnerabilities, and configuration changes.
* Reassess risks when significant changes occur.
* Report important changes to responsible stakeholders.

Expected output: Continuous monitoring plan.

4. Relationship to Other Frameworks

ISO/IEC 27001:2022

Used in this portfolio to explore Information Security Management Systems (ISMS), risk treatment, and applicable information security controls.

NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0

Used to organise cybersecurity outcomes into six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover.

NIST Risk Management Framework (RMF)

Used to structure system-level security and privacy risk management across seven lifecycle steps.

These frameworks complement one another but are not interchangeable. Their application depends on the organisation’s scope, requirements, and risk context.
